## Module Overview for Instructors

**Purpose of this module:**  
Who can nominate, and how DE reviews: accept, send back, or reject — plus who still owes what.

**Context (plain language):**

- What this hour is for: The nomination. Three reviews. Who finishes what.
- How it hooks to the hour before: 4.2 was sound, the shop list, and the close-the-loop note.
- How it hooks to the hour after: 4.4 is a tune request on a *live* rule. Same desk, different inbox.
- Why we are doing it this way: You wanted nominations as their own hour. The draft need not be perfect. The bar is “clear enough to review.”
- What we are *not* doing this hour: Writing a rule (1.3). Testing (4.2). Tunes (4.4). Packages (4.5). No lab. No DYA tickets.
- Extra step: none.

Say **accept for work**, **send back**, and **reject** the way the student guide does. **Sent back** in 4.2 is the close-the-loop *note*. Here **send back** is the review.

**Key Teaching Points:**
- Only SOC, hunt, and CTI nominate.
- Sketch is allowed. Bar is clear enough to review, not ready to deploy.
- Always say who still owes what.

## Detailed Teaching Notes

### 1. Key Concepts

Write the three reviews. Stop. Walk the three “given” lines.

If they invent a ticket: “Review, not the ticket.”  
If they start writing a rule: “That is 1.3.”  
If they treat “make a rule” as accept: “What activity? Send it back.”

---

## Knowledge Check – Answer Key

1. **Who can nominate?**  
   **Answer:** A SOC analyst, a hunter, or a CTI analyst.  
   **Explanation:** Outline a.

2. **Bar is “ready to deploy.” True or false?**  
   **Answer:** False. The bar is “clear enough to review.”  
   **Explanation:** Outline d.

3. **“Make a rule,” no activity?**  
   **Answer:** Send back. The nominator still owes the activity (what, where). DE does not finish a blank.  
   **Explanation:** Outline c and 4.3.1 tasks 1–2.
