## Module Overview for Instructors

**Purpose of this module:**  
Assign a letter and a number, then read the pair.

**Context (plain language):**

- What this hour is for: CTI analysts split who said it from whether this piece checks out.
- How it hooks to the hour before: 2.2.2 was the method. This hour is the source/info rating.
- How it hooks to the hour after: 2.2.4 names the bias that makes people skip the rating.
- Why we are doing it this way: Short 0.x / 4.x voice. Outline a–c. Standard Admiralty letters. Overlay a shop card if they have one.
- What we are *not* doing this hour: Likelihood words. Nation-state. Invent a new scale. No lab.
- Extra step: none.

**Key Teaching Points:**
- Two axes. A ≠ true. 1 ≠ trusted source.
- Internal is not automatically A1.

- Student Guide
- Slide Deck

## Detailed Teaching Notes

### 1. Key Concepts

Write A–F and 1–6. Walk internal Zeek vs anonymous blog.

If they say “likely”: “2.2.1.”  
If they A1 the blog: “F/E and 5/6.”  
If they A1 internals just because they are ours: “Usually reliable is B. Confirmed is 1 only if it matches.”

## Knowledge Check – Answer Key

1. **Reliable source means confirmed information. True or false?**  
   **Answer:** False. Two axes.  
   **Explanation:** Outline c.

2. **Two axes?**  
   **Answer:** Source reliability (A–F). Information credibility (1–6).  
   **Explanation:** Outline a–b.

3. **Anonymous blog, no internals. Rating?**  
   **Answer:** **F** or **E** and **5** or **6**. Cannot treat it as B2.  
   **Explanation:** Task 1.
