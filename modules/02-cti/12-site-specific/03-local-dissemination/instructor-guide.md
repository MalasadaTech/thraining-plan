## Module Overview for Instructors

**Purpose of this module:**  
Obtain this shop's customer and channel chart. Send using those names, or write that the chart is missing. Do not invent a distro. This closes 2.x.

**Context (plain language):**

- What this lesson is for: CTI analysts send a finished product to the people this shop actually serves, on the path this shop already named. A classroom audience and a classroom channel are not that list. This lesson is how you obtain this shop's named customers and named channels.
- How it hooks to the lesson before: 2.12.2 was the local produce / approve / archive path. The product is already drafted (2.11.1).
- How it hooks to the lesson after: Hunt 3.1 is why hunting exists. This lesson closes CTI 2.x.
- Why we are doing it this way: every shop has its own customers and channels. A new analyst obtains them. Do not invent a DYA roster. Classroom TLP and classroom channels stay in 2.11.2.
- What we are *not* doing in this lesson: inventing a customer list or a DYA channel list. Rewriting 2.11.2 as org policy. Teaching TLP or handling markings again. Environment / sensors (0.8). Hunt. No lab.
- Extra step: if you overlay a real shop chart, say it is overlay, not DYA policy. Do not invent names to fill the slide.

Use the same names as the student guide: **customers**, **internal**, **external**, **approved channel / method**, **chart**, and **dissemination** (sending the finished product). **A12** is the product being sent. Do not name who at DYA receives it.

**Key Teaching Points:**
- Internal and external customers are named on this shop's chart. Obtain the names. Do not guess them.
- The approved channel is the path the shop named. 2.11.2 is the classroom floor, not this shop's list.
- “I do not have the chart yet” is a passing answer. Inventing a distro fails. Missing the chart does not authorize SMS.

## Detailed Teaching Notes

### 1. Key Concepts

**Talking Points:**
- Open with the job: the product is finished, and you have to send it to this shop's people on this shop's path.
- Write internal customers, external customers, and approved channel. Stop. Do not fill a DYA roster on the board.
- 2.11.2 was classroom audience, channel, and TLP-style marking. Those names are not this shop's list. Do not reteach TLP today.
- Walk the two givens from the student guide. Chart shown: use the names on it for A12. No chart: write that you do not have it yet.
- If they invent a distro so the send can happen: that fails. Not yet is the answer.
- If they treat classroom IR / ticket from 2.11.2 as this shop's policy: that is the mix-up. Those were lesson stand-ins.
- If they send by SMS because there is no chart: unofficial paths still fail. That floor was 2.11.2.
- If they overlay a real shop chart: say it is overlay, not DYA policy. Use only the names on it.
- If they start hunt purpose: that is 3.1. This lesson closes CTI.

## Knowledge Check – Answer Key

1. **You should invent a DYA customer list so the product has recipients. True or false?**  
   **Answer:** False.  
   **Explanation:** Every shop has its own list. You obtain it. Inventing a DYA roster is not dissemination.

2. **How does this lesson differ from 2.11.2?**  
   **Answer:** 2.11.2 is the classroom floor: audience, an approved channel, and a handling marking. This lesson is this shop's named customers and named channels (or not yet).  
   **Explanation:** Classroom TLP and classroom channels are not org policy. Do not rewrite them as this shop's list.

3. **You have not been shown a chart. What do you write, and what channel do you still reject?**  
   **Answer:** **I do not have the chart yet.** Still reject personal SMS / private chat.  
   **Explanation:** Missing the local chart does not authorize an unofficial path. The SMS reject is the 2.11.2 classroom floor, not a DYA channel you invented.
