## Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

1. State that **production, approval, collection-request, and archive** paths are **local** and **not in this packet**.
2. Treat finding those paths as **early orientation**.
3. **Follow** the path you were shown — do not invent a workflow or skip approval because the draft was good.
4. **Archive** where the site says, not in a personal folder you made up.

**Mapped Proficiency Items:**
- K: 3.12.2 – Local production and approval processes
- T: 3.12.2.1 – Follow the local process for requesting collection or producing and approving products
- T: 3.12.2.2 – Document and archive intelligence products according to local standards

## 1. Key Concepts

**3.11.1** taught how to *draft*. **3.1.8** taught how to *plan* collection. **3.11.3** taught RFIs. This hour is the **site ticket / workflow** you must follow so a draft or a collection need becomes official.

This course **does not** name your approver, ticket queue, or archive. Every section builds its own. A new analyst asks and records the path.

**Workflow and authorities (outline a–b) — what exists, not the names:**

| Path | You will be shown (locally) | You do **not** do this hour |
|------|-----------------------------|-----------------------------|
| **Produce** | How a draft enters review | Rewrite the 3.11.1 quality table |
| **Approve** | Who must concur before send | Invent “the Harbor Chief stamps it” |
| **Request collection** | How to ask for collection you do not have | Write the collection *plan* (**3.1.8**) |
| **Archive** | Where the official copy lives | Personal Drive / Slack as the record |

**Orientation line:**  
`path (produce / approve / collect-request / archive) | who I asked | I have / do not have the local steps | next step`

**Follow line:**  
`what I want to do | path I was shown | I follow it / I still need the path / I invented a path (fail)`

If you have not been shown the path, you **do not send** and you **do not invent** one.

| This lesson | Other |
|-------------|-------|
| Find and follow the *site* path | Draft quality — **3.11.1** |
| Request collection the *local* way | Plan sources — **3.1.8** |
| Archive the official copy | TIP *search* — **3.3.1** |
| Not generic TLP send | **3.11.2** / **3.12.3** |

| Expected (usually) | Lead (usually) |
|--------------------|----------------|
| “Asked lead; no workflow in this packet; I will not send yet” | “Email the Harbor CTI Chief” as policy |
| Park the Night Owl note until the path is shown | “3.11.1 passed, so I can publish” |

## 2. Detailed Walkthrough / Examples

**Work on the desk:** a Night Owl activity note that already passed **3.11.1** quality.

### Example 1: Honest Orientation (Expected)

**Ask:** lead, first week — produce/approve/archive/collect-request.  
**Shown:** nothing in *this* packet.  
**Follow:** do not send the note; do not open a fake collection ticket. Get the path.

### Example 2: Invented Approval Chain (Lead)

**Draft:** “Harbor requires the CTI Chief and legal to stamp every note. I emailed both.”

**Fail.** You wrote a process this course does not own.  
**Lead:** Same sin as inventing PIR-1.

### Example 3: Skip Because the Draft Is Good (Lead)

**Draft:** Quality table passed, so publish to the intel DL now.

**Fail.** **3.11.1** is the draft. **This** hour is local approval. No path shown → no send.  
**Lead:** Good prose does not replace the local workflow.

## 3. Hands-On Exercise

**Objective:** Orient to the four paths. Follow only what you were shown. Do not invent a workflow.

**This packet contains no approval chain or archive path. That is intentional.**

**Instructions:**

1. One sentence each for Examples 1–3.
2. **Orient** (3.12.2.1–2): one **orientation line** each for produce, approve, collect-request, archive.
3. **Follow** (tasks): **follow lines** for:

   - A. Night Owl activity note is ready to become official.  
   - B. You need collection you do not have.  
   - C. “I’ll put the official copy in my personal folder.”  
   - D. “The local process is: email the Harbor Chief.”  

4. Do not write the product. Do not write a collection plan. Do not invent ticket names.
5. If the instructor overlays a real site workflow, follow *that* text only.

**Expected Outcome:**
- Three example summaries
- Four orientation lines (do not have the steps)
- Four follow lines (A/B = need the path; C/D = fail)
- No invented workflow
