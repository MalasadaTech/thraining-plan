## Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

1. State that **every org and section has its own** current PIRs / priorities, and they **change**.
2. Treat obtaining that list as an **early orientation task** — you do not invent it.
3. **Identify** current local priorities by asking and recording the *source*, not by writing a fake list.
4. **Align** work (including Night Owl facts already on your desk) to whatever requirement the site actually stated.

**Mapped Proficiency Items:**
- K: 3.12.1 – Local intelligence requirements and priorities
- T: 3.12.1.1 – Identify current local priorities and align analytic work to them

## 1. Key Concepts

**3.1.4** taught what a PIR *is* and how to write one. This hour you **do not write PIRs**. You **find the ones your section is using now** and put your analysis against them.

SOC environment facts are **1.8**. Collection *planning* is **3.1.8**. Finished products are **3.11.1**. This curriculum **does not** publish a Harbor PIR list. If an instructor overlays a real site list in class, use *that*. Until then, the honest fill is “I do not have the local list yet.”

**Local and unique (outline a):**

| Fact | What it means for a new analyst |
|------|----------------------------------|
| Every org / section posts **its own** current PIRs or priorities | Do not reuse another unit’s list, a vendor PDF, or a training example as policy |
| The list is **time-bounded** | Last quarter’s PIR may be retired. Ask for *current* |
| You are not the author on day one | Obtaining the list is **orientation**, like a badge and a TIP login |

**Where you obtain it (ask; do not invent the store name as policy):** your lead / supervisor, the local intel store or share your lead points at, the incoming brief. Record **who pointed** and **where**. If no one has shown you a list, you **do not have** current local priorities.

**How they drive focus (outline b):** a PIR tells you what is **in focus**, what to **park**, and when to **ask** before you spend a week on an interesting thread (Night Owl included). Interesting ≠ assigned.

**Orientation line (task 1):**  
`who I asked | where they pointed | I have / do not have the current list | next step`

**Align line (task 2):**  
`work on my desk | compared to the list I was actually shown | in focus / parked / I still need the list`

You cannot mark Night Owl “in focus” if you never obtained the list.

## 2. Detailed Walkthrough / Examples

**Work on the desk (not a PIR):** Night Owl on WS-JLEE — encoded PowerShell, Run key, `nightowl-updates.net`.

### Example 1: Honest Orientation (Expected)

**Ask:** your lead, first week.  
**Shown:** nothing in *this* curriculum (and no overlay yet).  
**Orientation line:** `lead | incoming brief / local store they will name | do not have the current list | get the list before I open a new Night Owl line`  
**Align:** `Night Owl facts | no list shown | I still need the list`

### Example 2: Invented PIR (Lead)

**Draft:** “Harbor PIR-1 is nation-state targeting of finance. Night Owl is in focus.”

**Fail.** You wrote a requirement this course does not own and treated it as policy.  
**Lead:** Inventing the list is not “identifying” it.

### Example 3: Interesting but Unchecked (Lead)

**Draft:** Continue a week of Night Owl pivots because the story is good. Never asked what the section cares about this month.

**Fail.** Local requirements drive focus (**outline b**). Unchecked work is unaligned.  
**Lead:** Ask first. Then align or park.

## 3. Hands-On Exercise

**Objective:** Orient. Do not invent PIRs. Align only against a list you were actually shown.

**This packet contains no local PIR list. That is intentional.**

**Instructions:**

1. One sentence each for Examples 1–3.
2. **Identify** (task 1): write an **orientation line** for you as a new arrival (who you would ask; that you do not have the list in this packet).
3. **Align** (task 2): **align lines** for:

   - A. Night Owl on WS-JLEE — you have **not** been shown a local list.  
   - B. Your lead *in class* reads a real site PIR (if they do). Align Night Owl to *that* text only. If they do not, write “still need the list.”  
   - C. “I will use last course’s example PIR as this section’s list.”

4. Do not write a PIR. Do not draft a **3.11.1** product. Do not plan collection (**3.1.8**).
5. Invented Harbor PIR text is a fail.

**Expected Outcome:**
- Three example summaries
- One orientation line (do not have the list)
- Three align lines (A = need the list; C = fail)
- No invented PIR
