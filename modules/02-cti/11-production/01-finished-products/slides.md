### Slide 2 – Why this lesson exists
**Title:** Why this lesson exists

CTI analysts write a **finished product** so someone can use the judged answer.

Collection and notes do not help until that answer is on the page to a standard.  
A TIP paste is not that product.

**Speaker Notes:**  
This slide is the student intro. Name types, required elements, and quality standards before anyone picks a channel or invents a government. Dissemination is the next lesson.

---

### Slide 3 – Types of finished products
**Title:** Types of finished products

**Assessment** — a judged answer to a named question.  
**Profile** — a short picture of an activity cluster, not a country you invented.  
**RFI response** — a finished answer to a request for information.

Pick **one** type that matches the requirement.

**Speaker Notes:**  
An RFI response is a product type. How to run the RFI queue is 2.11.3. Do not staple all three types into one dump.

---

### Slide 4 – Structure and required elements
**Title:** What must be in it

**Question** — the requirement the product answers.  
**What you know** — sourced facts.  
**Judgment** — an estimative term, not “could be.”  
**So-what** — who can act, and on what.  
**Confidence / caveat** — how good the evidence is, and what you do not know.

**Speaker Notes:**  
Walk the five elements once. Audience rewrite is 2.1.6. Likelihood wording depth is 2.2.1. Stay on the product structure today.

---

### Slide 5 – Quality and analytic standards
**Title:** What a draft must meet

It answers the named requirement.  
It names its sources.  
It uses an estimative term.  
It does not invent a country.

A hash dump fails. A TIP paste fails.  
A vendor “APT” name is a label, not a government.

**Speaker Notes:**  
These are the checks used on the A12 draft in the next slide. If they start listing channels or TLP, that is 2.11.2.
