### Slide 2 – Why this lesson exists
**Title:** Why this lesson exists

A CTI analyst is asked to brief or hand off what landed on the desk.

Before you treat it as a decision, know **what layer** it is.

This lesson is that sort.

**Speaker Notes:**  
This slide is the student intro. Name the layer so they do not brief a raw hash as intelligence. Do not teach the lifecycle, PIR format, or a finished paper today.

---

### Slide 3 – Three terms
**Title:** Data, information, intelligence

**Data** — a raw fact as recorded.  
**Information** — that fact with context (who / what / when / where).  
**Intelligence** — that story judged against a question, with a so-what.

Information describes. Intelligence judges.

**Speaker Notes:**  
One line each. Stop. Renaming a feed “intel” does not change the layer. If they ask about a PIR, the question can be informal; PIR format is 2.1.4.

---

### Slide 4 – The path, not a rename
**Title:** Data becomes information, then intelligence

Add **context**, then add a **judgment** against a question.

The question can be informal.  
If you are unsure, it is not intelligence yet.

**Speaker Notes:**  
Walk the path. The product of this lesson is the layer, not a lifecycle stage. Lifecycle is 2.1.2.

---

### Slide 5 – Same case, three layers
**Title:** Same case, three layers

**Data** — `203.0.113.88`. Or the hash of Temp `invoice.vbs`.  
**Information** — that IP is the A record for the domain that served `update.exe`; file on **WS-JLEE**.  
**Intelligence** — we assess it is the payload host for incident **A12**; treat it as such.

**Speaker Notes:**  
Walk the three givens from the student guide. The product is the layer, not the incident plot. Do not dump the Run key. Do not write a 2.11 paper.
