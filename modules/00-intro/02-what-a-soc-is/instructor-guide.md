## Module Overview for Instructors

**Purpose of this module:**  
Get everyone to the same three facts: what a SOC is, that more than one job sits next to it, and that DYA / PRD are course fiction.

**Context (plain language):**

- What this hour is for: Name the SOC so later hours can talk about desks and hand-offs without arguing about the word.
- How it hooks to the hour before: There is no earlier lesson. This is the first hour of the course.
- How it hooks to the hour after: Next we name each job in one sentence (0.3). Then how work moves (0.4).
- Why we are doing it this way: You asked for a short front door, and to review one small piece at a time. This hour is only “what it is.”
- What we are *not* doing this hour: The hand-off chain, job one-liners, overlap, course map, companion story, Zeek, ATT&CK, tickets, PIR lists. No lab.
- Extra step: none.

Use **SOC**, **DYA**, **PRD**. If you mention a write-up, say **report**.

**Key Teaching Points:**
- Watch and **start** the response — not every security job.
- Team sport = more than one job nearby. Names of those jobs are 0.3.
- DYA / PRD = this course’s story names, not site policy.

## Knowledge Check – Answer Key

1. **What is a SOC?**  
   **Answer:** A place that watches for bad or suspicious activity and starts the response.  
   **Explanation:** Outline a.

2. **Team sport?**  
   **Answer:** More than one job sits in or next to the SOC.  
   **Explanation:** Outline b.

3. **DYA and PRD?**  
   **Answer:** Course fiction — Dixon, Yamada, & Associates and Pink River Dolphin. Not site policy.  
   **Explanation:** Outline c.
