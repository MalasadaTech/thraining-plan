## Module Overview for Instructors

**Purpose of this module:**  
Obtain the shop’s hunt documentation standard — required elements, and where and how hunts are documented — then write there, or write that the standard is missing. Do not invent a DYA template or archive path.

**Context (plain language):**

- What this lesson is for: Hunters write the hunt on the shop’s form, in the shop’s official place, so the next person can find a complete record. Obtain the local documentation standard, then write there.
- How it hooks to the lesson before: 3.7.1 obtained how a hunt is initiated, controlled, and how leads are managed.
- How it hooks to the lesson after: 3.7.3 is what a finished hunt must produce and who it is handed to.
- Why we are doing it this way: every site has its own form and official place. The classroom hunt card is already 3.2.2. This lesson is the local standard, not a rewrite of that card, and not a DYA template this course publishes.
- What we are *not* doing in this lesson: inventing a DYA hunt template or archive path, promoting personal notes or Slack as the official record, initiate/control (3.7.1), outputs and hand-off (3.7.3), no lab.
- Extra step: none. If you overlay a real shop form, say it is overlay for the room, not DYA policy.

Use the same names as the student guide: **required elements**, **where and how** hunts are documented (the **store** is the official place, named on first use), **obtain-and-follow**, and **I do not have the local standard yet.** The **3.2.2** classroom card (hypothesis, scope, priority, unique pattern) is training, not the site form.

**Key Teaching Points:**
- The local standard is obtained, not invented. This course does not publish DYA’s template or path.
- Required elements and where/how hunts are documented both vary by site.
- “I do not have the local standard yet” is a passing answer. Scratch notes are not the hunt record.

**Common Student Challenges:**
- Treat the 3.2.2 classroom card as the site form. Why: that lesson already taught a card. Example: filing hypothesis / scope / priority / pattern as if it were shop policy.
- Declare a scratch note the official record. Why: they wrote something down. Example: calling a personal note or a Slack thread “the hunt record.”

## Detailed Teaching Notes

### 1. Key Concepts

**Talking Points:**
- Open with the job: the hunt has to live on the shop’s form, in the shop’s official place, so the next person can find a complete record. The first move is to obtain that standard.
- 3.7.1 was initiate, control, and leads. Today is how the hunt is written down.
- The 3.2.2 classroom card is hypothesis, scope, priority, and unique pattern. It is training. It is not the site form.
- Walk required elements: what the site form always wants. You obtain that list. You do not write one for DYA.
- Walk where and how: the official place and method. Your lead names it. Personal notes and Slack are not that record. Do not invent a folder path.
- Walk obtain-and-follow. If you overlay a real shop form, name it as overlay, not DYA policy.
- Walk the two products from the student guide: obtain the elements and the official place, or write “I do not have the local standard yet.” Document only on what you were shown.
- If they paste the 3.2.2 card as policy: that is the training card, not the site form. Fail that product.
- If they invent a template name or a folder path so the page is not blank: that is invented. Fail that product.
- If they start an initiate path or a recipient list: that is 3.7.1 / 3.7.3.

## Knowledge Check – Answer Key

1. **The 3.2.2 classroom card is the shop’s official hunt form. True or false?**  
   **Answer:** False.  
   **Explanation:** The classroom card is training. Every shop has its own form. This course does not publish DYA’s. Obtain the local standard; do not treat the card as policy.

2. **What two things does this lesson obtain?**  
   **Answer:** Required elements of hunt documentation. Where and how hunts are documented.  
   **Explanation:** Both vary by site. You obtain them from the role or place your lead names. You do not invent a template or a path.

3. **What do you write if no one has shown you the standard?**  
   **Answer:** **I do not have the local standard yet.** Do not declare a personal note the hunt record.  
   **Explanation:** Documenting a hunt starts with obtaining the standard. “Not yet” is a pass. Inventing a template or treating scratch notes as the record is not.
