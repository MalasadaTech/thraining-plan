## Teaching Notes

- Reframe this as governance onboarding rather than repeated 'do not invent' warnings.
- Map initiation, scope authority, pause/stop, escalation and lead ownership.
- Lead management keeps a hunt bounded without losing useful findings.
- If process is missing, record the specific onboarding gap and owner/source needed.

## Common Coaching Pattern

When a learner overstates the evidence, ask:

1. **What did we actually observe?**
2. **What does that observation support?**
3. **What additional evidence would be required for the stronger claim?**

For hunt modules, also ask whether the required telemetry exists and whether the search is bounded enough for a negative result to mean anything.

## Knowledge Check – Answer Key

### 1. Why can scope expansion require approval?

**Expected answer:** Scope expansion can materially change resources, data access, operational coordination, and incident interaction, so local governance may require authorization.

### 2. What happens to out-of-scope lead?

**Expected answer:** Record the lead with evidence/provenance and route it through the local lead-triage/ownership process instead of silently expanding scope.

### 3. Missing governance path: what record?

**Expected answer:** Local hunt-governance path not yet verified; identify the process owner/source needed.

## Transition

Use the student's **Next** line to connect this lesson to the following module. Preserve unresolved visibility, detection, attribution, and scope gaps instead of solving them with assumptions.
